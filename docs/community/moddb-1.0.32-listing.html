Why this exists

This was made for me. It was made for singleplayer. I wanted something like Minecraft Distant Horizons in Vintage Story. What was on the market was not good enough for how I play. I had the tools, so I forked Vintage Horizons (AliasFactory, MIT) and built the version I actually want to look at. Everybody else is along for that if they want to be.

If you can improve it, take it, or change it, I do not care. If the game is better at the end of the day, that is all I care about. Anybody can have the credit. There is no credit due to me.

Why make a mod? Same reason anybody makes anything. There was a void in the marketplace, and I had the skills to fill it. That is it.

What this is

Far terrain past vanilla view distance. A persistent level-of-detail cache from chunks your client already has. Real 3D land, trees, and builds you already saw. Not a fog silhouette. I am not trying to replace Horizons. Credit stays with AliasFactory and everyone else who is actually owed it.

What works for me may not work for you. Different worlds, hardware, and servers behave differently. If Horizons or another LOD mod already does what you need, use that.

Recommended: Client MeshRef Leak Fix

I run this with Client MeshRef Leak Fix (vsvaogc). It is client-only. A dedicated server does not need it. Distant Vistas does not require it to load.

Singleplayer vs client-side

  • Singleplayer: install on the client. This is what I built it for and what I test.
  • Client-side: it draws from what the client already loaded. You do not need a server install for that. That is the point.
  • Vanilla public servers: from 1.0.1 onward the join overlay is skipped so hop teleports and gamemode commands do not kick you off. Far land fills in as you walk, same as before the overlay era.
  • Server assist: exists if you put it on a server too. That is not an official feature I care about. I did not make this for multiplayer first.

Latest: 1.0.32 (official)

This is the official 1.0 line through 1.0.32. Far 3D land that stays. Seasons and frost on far terrain. Join overlay paints the horizon while you stay at spawn. Drop distantvistas_1.0.32.zip in Mods. Do not extract. Fully quit Vintage Story, then start it again. Leaving to the menu does not load a new zip.

What changed since 1.0.0 (the big stuff, in plain English):

  • Login overlay without moving you. Older builds teleported you cell to cell. From 1.0.24 onward that paint-revision wedge is gone. From 1.0.27 onward scout workers stream and bake around visit cells while you stay at your pickup spot. From 1.0.30 onward those scouts are real player-style stream centers. You should land back at the same X/Y/Z and look direction you had when the overlay started.
  • Faster login bake (1.0.31–1.0.32). Sixteen scouts run in parallel (eight wait for near meshes, eight paint farther out). The overlay should show 16/16 scouts within seconds and the percent bar should move every few seconds. 1.0.32 adds less garbage during colour sampling, reuses scratch buffers, caches texture means per block type, and SIMD speed-up on the work after colour sampling (not inside the game’s own GetColor calls). Progress ETA is batch-aware instead of pretending one tick equals one old hop.
  • Bigger horizon fill. Coverage disk now reaches the Farseer silhouette band (~4075 blocks from spawn at the 750 graphics hold). FlagBaked land should meet the far skyline instead of stopping in a thin ring.
  • Farseer look I actually liked (since ~1.0.23). Gray tent / smoke body with black mountain tips, not a void and not a white sky wash. Readable ridges from 1.0.28. Keep Farseer SkyTint around 0.4 in Ctrl+Shift+F — cranked values bleach hills into the sky. 1.0.32 pulls the smoke to the meshed rim when meshes lag so you should not see a white sky strip between our land and Farseer.
  • Pause-on-Start. If you use Pause-on-Start, the overlay still ticks while the splash is up; when it finishes (or you Esc out), pause and the menu come back the way you expect.
  • Seasons and frost (carried forward + tightened). Overlay and walking share one bake from the live calendar month. Summer is bright green. Winter without a snow sheet is mottled brown, tan, and olive — texture specks, not manila plates, not a fake white sheet. Real snow stays snow. Autumn trees and bushes keep species colour on far LOD; frost hits sides and crown tops in late autumn and winter. /time while you are already in the world does not retint — change month, then rejoin. After 30 days, or a later calendar month, the overlay can recapture.
  • Visited land stays. Land you already walked does not turn into sky when you leave. I claimed this more than once; 0.7.49 is when it actually held, and the 1.0 line kept it.
  • View-distance sky circle. LOD only steps aside when that tile is actually loaded — not a moving cookie-cutter arc of blue sky.
  • Mountains. Ridges no longer chop down into cave floors at the LOD ring (mip merge fix from the 0.7 line, kept).
  • Spawn plates. Looking down at spawn no longer paints a low-poly plate over the real ground.
  • Clouds. Cloud tiles stretch out to the LOD horizon so you do not get a nearby cloud box floating over far mountains (1.0.12).
  • Farseer coexistence. Farseer used to make this mod not render at all. That is fixed. We draw with Farseer in the background. Our tiles sit on top where we have them. We overlay their region shaders without Harmony patches. ChunkLOD and TopoHorizon still make us sit out unless .dvdefer off.

After you join, check: overlay shows 16/16 scouts and percent moves; when it ends you are at the same spot; no holes underfoot; far land meets gray/black Farseer silhouette without a white gap; FPS comes back (graphics slider and overdraw settings restored); walking still captures new land on vanilla servers.

Why a fork, and what is different

Same starting point as Vintage Horizons: a persistent LOD cache from chunks the client already received. After that the draw path is different.

  • Parent plates. Horizons will mesh a coarse parent as coverage when children are not ready. From the air that is giant square plates with sheer walls. Distant Vistas does not use a parent box as a stand-in. Incomplete L0 stays off screen until it is real land.
  • Trail behind the camera. This build keeps captured land in the draw set behind and beside the camera. Fill is not allowed to stall those tiles just because the window moved.
  • Vanilla handoff. Vanilla-owns is 3D plus look-down, not a horizontal disc. Looking down from altitude still draws LOD where vanilla has no chunks.
  • Night color. Stored albedo is multiplied by the same live ambient the chunk shaders use.
  • Season. Climate tint is sampled into a slow table. Season is a live shader clock from the calendar, mixed onto vegetation. Rock, snow, and water are not forced into fake autumn grey-green when you walk away from vanilla range.
  • Vanilla edge fade. Vanilla chunk shaders fade alpha at the view-distance ring. Those vertex shaders are overridden so LOD joins behind at full alpha instead of a fog slice.
  • Farseer. We do not Harmony-patch Farseer. We draw after it. Their heightmap can stay as a cheap backdrop. Ours is the real far terrain.

In-game commands

  • .dvistas — status, meshes, far edge, server assist
  • .dvdetail [blocks] — detail halving distance (default 512; try 1024 for sharper far terrain, costs more)
  • .dvfar <blocks> — cap LOD distance (0 = unlimited)
  • .dvdefer [on|off] — defer when ChunkLOD or TopoHorizon draws (restart to apply). Farseer no longer uses this path.
